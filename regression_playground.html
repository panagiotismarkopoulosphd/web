Theory

Polynomial regression and the bias–variance trade-off.

Data-generating process

Observations follow

$$ y = f(x) + e $$

where \( f \) is a fixed polynomial of degree \( d_f \) (unknown to the learner), and the noise \( e \) satisfies \( \mathbb{E}[e] = 0 \) and is independent of \( x \). Noise variance is \( \sigma^2 \). Inputs \( x \) are drawn uniformly from \( [-1, 1] \).

The coefficients of the true polynomial are fixed once \( d_f \) is selected, so the ground-truth function remains constant while you experiment with models and sample sizes.

The model

We approximate \( f \) by a polynomial of degree \( d_m \):

$$ m(x;\mathbf{a}) = a_0 + a_1 x + \cdots + a_{d_m} x^{d_m} $$

The parameter vector \( \mathbf{a} \) is obtained by the exact least-squares solution \( \mathbf{a} = X^{+} \mathbf{y} \), where \( X^{+} \) is the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse of the Vandermonde matrix (computed via SVD). No regularization is applied. The fitted model is \( \hat{f}(x) = m(x;\hat{\mathbf{a}}) \).

True degree \( d_f \) and model degree \( d_m \) are chosen independently. When \( d_m < d_f \) the model is misspecified (underfitting). When \( d_m > d_f \) the extra coefficients can fit noise (overfitting).

What the axes mean

Main plot

  • Horizontal axis: input feature \( x \in [-1, 1] \).
  • Vertical axis: response \( y \).

Blue markers: one training set of size \( N \). Black dashed curve: true polynomial \( f \). Orange curve: least-squares fit of degree \( d_m \). Optional green band: mean prediction ± one standard deviation across independent training sets.

Bottom plot

  • Horizontal axis: model degree \( d_m \).
  • Vertical axis: bias², variance, and bias² + variance + \( \sigma^2 \), each averaged over \( x \).

Bias–variance decomposition

For a fixed \( x \),

$$ \mathbb{E}\bigl[(y - \hat{f}(x))^2\bigr] = \bigl(\mathbb{E}[\hat{f}(x)] - f(x)\bigr)^2 + \mathrm{Var}(\hat{f}(x)) + \sigma^2 $$

The expectation and variance are over the random training set.

  • Bias² is large when \( d_m \) is too small relative to \( d_f \) (the average fit cannot recover the true polynomial).
  • Variance grows with \( d_m \) because higher-degree fits are more sensitive to the particular training sample.
  • \( \sigma^2 \) is irreducible.

Test error is typically minimized near \( d_m = d_f \).

Monte Carlo estimation

Bias and variance are estimated by drawing \( R \) independent training sets, fitting a degree-\( d_m \) polynomial on each, evaluating the fits on a dense grid, and computing sample mean and variance of the predictions at each grid point. Bias² is the squared difference between the mean prediction and true \( f(x) \). Results are averaged over the grid.

Train MSE versus test MSE

  • Train MSE is computed on the points used for fitting; it decreases as \( d_m \) increases.
  • Test MSE is computed on a large independent test set; it reflects generalization and exhibits the classic U-shape.
